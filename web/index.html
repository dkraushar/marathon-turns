    How this works

    The geometry: run parallel to the inside line, w feet farther out, and a turn of angle θ costs you w × θ extra feet (θ in radians), however tight the corner. A 90° turn on a 60-ft street is about +94 ft. Running all the way into the far corner instead costs 2w·tan(θ/2).

    The data: the course is rebuilt street by street on NYC’s LION street centerlines from the race-day road-closure list, including 2026’s new Bronx route. Widths are LION curb-to-curb widths, minus 1 ft off each curb. Divided roads use one roadbed, since you can’t cut across the median.

    The data: the course comes from a GPS track of the race, snapped onto OpenStreetMap streets. OpenStreetMap rarely records curb-to-curb width, so widths are estimated from lane counts (8 ft per lane + 30 ft for parking, bike lanes and gutters), a rule fitted to NYC’s measured widths. It’s rough street by street (±13 ft) but identical for every city, so the races compare fairly. Cities with less street parking than New York probably come out a little wide.

    A ceiling, not a prediction. Barricades, water stations and tens of thousands of other runners change the usable width. Bay Ridge corral variants are simplified to one route.
