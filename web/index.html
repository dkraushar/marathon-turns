What if you took every turn wide?

DK Dana Kraushar LinkedIn ↗ · GitHub ↗

The course measures 26.2 miles along the tightest possible line, hugging every corner. Nobody runs that line. Here’s the ceiling: what you would run if you swung around the outside of all – turns instead of the inside.

26.22mi
+0 ft beyond the certified distance

Smooth arc: stay the same distance off the inside line and swing around the corner in a curve, like a car taking a wide turn. The realistic worst case.

Corner to corner: run straight into the far corner of the intersection, then turn. This would be a bizarre way to run; the theoretical maximum.

    How this works

    The geometry: run parallel to the inside line, w feet farther out, and a turn of angle θ costs you w × θ extra feet (θ in radians), however tight the corner. A 90° turn on a 60-ft street is about +94 ft. Running all the way into the far corner instead costs 2w·tan(θ/2).

    The data: the course is rebuilt street by street on NYC’s LION street centerlines from the race-day road-closure list, including 2026’s new Bronx route. Widths are LION curb-to-curb widths, minus 1 ft off each curb. Divided roads use one roadbed, since you can’t cut across the median.

    A ceiling, not a prediction. Barricades, water stations and 50,000 other runners change the usable width. Bay Ridge corral variants are simplified to one route.
